What this research is about

Cities are increasingly modelled digitally — every building stored not just as a shape, but with meaning attached: which surfaces are walls, which are roofs, which touch the ground. These semantic 3D city models drive energy simulation, solar-potential studies and planning tools.

Creating hypothetical buildings for such models is still largely manual, which limits how many planning scenarios anyone can explore. This thesis trains a generative machine-learning model to synthesise new buildings directly, converted from open CityGML city data into a 3D grid of labelled cubes (voxels) at half-metre resolution. You ask for a building of a certain type, height and roof, and the model produces one that has never existed.

Why this study exists

The model is already measured automatically on many things — how realistic the geometry is, whether surfaces are correctly labelled, and whether a requested roof type actually changes the shape produced. Those are numbers computed by software.

What no automatic measure can tell us is whether the result reads as the right building to a person. A model can score well on every metric and still produce something a human would not recognise. This study is the part of the evaluation that only people can provide.

Your answers are compared against the same task on real buildings from city data. That real-building score is the benchmark: it shows how legibly a roof type can be read from this kind of 3D model at all. If the generated buildings are as readable as the real ones, the model expresses roof types the way it should.

What you will do

  1. You see one building at a time, rendered as 3D blocks.
  2. You pick the roof type it appears to have, from a list with diagrams.
  3. If you cannot tell, choose none of the above or skip it — both are useful, and guessing wildly is not.

Some buildings are real and some are machine-generated. You are not told which, and it makes no difference to the task. There is no feedback between items — just go with your instinct.
